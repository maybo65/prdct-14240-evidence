--- LOCK FOOTPRINT held on the assets objects during the write (pg_locks, this pid) ---
 relation | assets                       | AccessShareLock  | t
 relation | assets                       | RowExclusiveLock | t   <- table-level INTENT lock
 relation | asset_app_definition_id      | RowExclusiveLock | t   (+ AccessShareLock)
 relation | asset_status                 | RowExclusiveLock | t   (+ AccessShareLock)

 ... (every assets index: AccessShareLock + RowExclusiveLock for index maintenance) ...

  The UPDATE takes only a table-level RowExclusiveLock (intent) on `assets` plus the
  per-index RowExclusiveLock that ordinary DML index maintenance requires. It never
  escalates to ACCESS EXCLUSIVE and never issues LOCK TABLE, so writers to any OTHER
  tenant/app-definition row are never blocked. Row-level exclusive locks are taken
  ONLY on the rows the index scan actually reaches (the sanctioned family); Postgres
  records those in the tuple xmax, not as separate pg_locks rows. Ascending-id chunk
  order makes row-lock acquisition deterministic across concurrent sanction toggles
  (no lock-order deadlock).
